# Continental European voter-information and voting-advice tools: what they do well and badly

Deep dive for What's It To Me? (whatsittome.org). Written 25 September 2026. All pages were accessed on 25 September 2026 unless stated. Every fact below is tagged OBSERVED (evidence quoted or paraphrased from a named source) or JUDGE (my opinion). Where I could not verify something it says "unverified".

A note on method. The session's web-search budget ran out part-way through, so later pages were found through Google News RSS, Wikipedia reference lists and the sites' own navigation, then fetched directly. A handful of sites are blocked from this environment (Le Monde, Helsingin Sanomat, El Mundo, RTL, Trouw, Euractiv, Wiley), which limits what I could confirm on Le Monde's programme comparator, HS's vaalikone and the closure of VoteWatch Europe. The gaps are marked.

Vocabulary: a VAA ("voting advice application") is a quiz that matches your answers to parties or candidates and ranks them. What's It To Me? is deliberately not a VAA. Most of the tools below are; the lessons at the end are about the parts that transfer to a site that never ranks.

## 1. Germany

### 1.1 Wahl-O-Mat (bpb)

Who runs it and funding. OBSERVED: run by the Bundeszentrale für politische Bildung (bpb), the federal civic-education agency, since 2002. Source: bpb, "Wahl-O-Mat", https://www.bpb.de/themen/wahl-o-mat/. Funding is not stated on the pages I read; it is a federal agency product, so tax-funded (JUDGE, and "unverified" for any figure).

What it shows. OBSERVED: 38 theses; answer options "stimme zu", "neutral", "stimme nicht zu", "These überspringen"; after answering, users can mark theses that "zählen doppelt bei der Berechnung" (count double); results as bar charts of "das Ausmaß der Übereinstimmung"; a "Begründungen" view where each party's own justification is shown per thesis; a "Tuning" view to change answers and see the effect. Source: bpb, "Wie funktioniert der Wahl-O-Mat?", https://www.bpb.de/themen/wahl-o-mat/294576/wie-funktioniert-der-wahl-o-mat/.

How positions are sourced. OBSERVED: pure party self-placement. "Die Antworten auf die Thesen stammen direkt von den Parteien." Parties get the 80–100 candidate theses through a secure online system and "zwei bis drei Wochen Zeit, alle Thesen zu beantworten"; they can add a justification per thesis; political scientists check answers for inconsistencies and misunderstandings and parties may revise, but parties keep the last word and answers are "presented directly without interpretation". Source: bpb, "Die Entstehung eines Wahl-O-Mat", https://www.bpb.de/themen/wahl-o-mat/45292/die-entstehung-eines-wahl-o-mat/.

How theses are chosen (the youth editorial team). OBSERVED: an editorial team of "20 bis 25 Jung- und Erstwählerinnen und -wählern" aged 16/18 to 26 from the relevant state or the whole country, recruited by open online application; a three-day workshop about three months before the election drafts 80–100 theses from party programmes in thematic sub-groups (labour, energy, education, finance, governance); after party answers come back, the team cuts to 38 theses chosen for importance, controversy, ability to tell parties apart, and topic spread. Source: same bpb page. For the 2019 European election the FAQ says 24 young people (18–26) with experts and the bpb chose the theses, "parties were not involved in the final selection", 81 theses were sent to parties and reduced to 38 by statistical analysis; the FAQ also says the tool is "ein Angebot zur politischen Bildung und Information, keine Wahlempfehlung" and that about 10 per cent of users get a result they did not expect. Source: bpb, "Häufig gestellte Fragen, Europawahl 2019", https://www.bpb.de/themen/wahl-o-mat/europawahl-2019/288391/haeufig-gestellte-fragen/.

Party inclusion. OBSERVED: for the 2019 European election, "all parties approved by the Federal Returning Officer" could take part regardless of classification by the domestic-intelligence agencies (same FAQ). For the 2025 Bundestag election the bpb page lists "29 Parteien". Source: https://www.bpb.de/themen/wahl-o-mat/bundestagswahl-2025/.

Court cases. OBSERVED:
- 20 May 2019, Verwaltungsgericht Köln, on an application by Volt Deutschland: the results page let users compare themselves with at most eight parties at once while 14 parties were in the European Parliament; the court found "factual disadvantage to smaller or lesser-known parties" contrary to Art. 21(1) with Art. 3(1) Grundgesetz and prohibited operating the tool "in its current form"; the bpb switched it off the same evening ("Wir schalten umgehend ab"). Sources: LTO, "VG Köln: Der Wahl-O-Mat ist verfassungswidrig", https://www.lto.de/recht/nachrichten/n/wahl-o-mat-europawahl-2019-verfassungwidrig-parteien-chancengleichheit; t-online, "Kurz vor Europawahl 2019: Wahl-O-Mat geht wegen Gericht offline", https://www.t-online.de/nachrichten/europawahl/id_85788160/kurz-vor-europawahl-2019-wahl-o-mat-geht-wegen-gericht-offline.html.
- 23 May 2019 settlement: bpb agreed that in future "the results page will no longer limit selection to a maximum of 8 parties" and would add "the option to select all parties at once via a single button"; the tool went back online. Source: bpb press release, "Wahl-O-Mat zur Europawahl wieder online", https://www.bpb.de/die-bpb/presse/pressemitteilungen/291808/wahl-o-mat-zur-europawahl-wieder-online/.
- 10 March 2021, VG Köln, case 6 L 385/21: an independent candidate in Stuttgart I asked to be included in the Baden-Württemberg Wahl-O-Mat; refused, because an independent stands in one constituency only so "his candidacy for the overwhelming majority of the Wahl-O-Mat target group [was] without significance" and different treatment of individuals and parties is "constitutionally not required". Source: VG Köln press release, https://www.vg-koeln.nrw.de/behoerde/presse/Pressemitteilungen/Archiv/2021/10_10032021/index.php.

Usage figures. OBSERVED: Bundestagswahl 2021: 21,251,706 uses between 2 and 26 September 2021, against 15,693,732 in 2017 (bpb press release 27 Sep 2021, https://www.bpb.de/die-bpb/presse/pressemitteilungen/340868/wahl-o-mat-so-haeufig-genutzt-wie-nie-zuvor/). Bundestagswahl 2025: "26,0 Mio. Mal genutzt"; Europawahl 2024: 14.8 million; Bayern 2023: 2.95 million; Hessen 2023: 1.31 million; NRW 2022: 1.47 million; Niedersachsen 2022: 904,000; Baden-Württemberg 2026: 2.15 million; Sachsen-Anhalt 2026: 1.28 million; Berlin 2026: 1.05 million; "rund 165 Millionen Mal" in total since 2002 (bpb archive page, https://www.bpb.de/themen/wahl-o-mat/45484/wie-entsteht-der-wahl-o-mat/ and https://www.bpb.de/themen/wahl-o-mat/).

Criticism. OBSERVED: a September 2026 Euronews piece summarises current criticisms: an analysis found only 10 of 38 theses "ohne zusätzliche Annahmen klar zu beantworten"; every thesis has equal weight whether "symbolische Verwaltungsfrage oder eine weitreichende Grundrechtsentscheidung"; "zwei völlig gegensätzliche Parteien können denselben Näherungswert erhalten"; the tool works as a "Konfliktfilter" that hides consensus topics; and it says nothing about the "demokratische Qualität oder Verfassungstreue einer Partei". Source: Euronews, "Millionen nutzen Wahl-O-Mat: Aber er kann auch in die Irre führen", 15 Sep 2026, https://de.euronews.com/2026/09/15/wahl-o-mat-wahlen-berlin-mecklenburg-vorpommern.

Design features worth borrowing (URL: https://www.bpb.de/themen/wahl-o-mat/294576/wie-funktioniert-der-wahl-o-mat/). JUDGE: the "Begründungen" page (party's own words per thesis, side by side) is the best part and is not a ranking. The 2019 settlement's "all parties with one click" is a good default for any list of candidates. The youth editorial workshop is a repeatable, documented process for choosing what to ask.

What went wrong. JUDGE: two courts in three years, both about who is in and who is out. The eight-party cap was a UI convenience that became a constitutional problem. Any tool that lists some contenders and not others must have written, published criteria and must apply them to everyone on the ballot.

### 1.2 Abgeordnetenwatch.de (Parlamentwatch e.V.)

Who and funding. OBSERVED: run by the association Parlamentwatch e.V.; "über 12.500 Förder:innen" (regular donors) as of December 2025; 32 staff plus 12 freelancers; OLIN gGmbH gave €85,000 in 2013–2016 to help set up the non-profit; member of the Initiative Transparente Zivilgesellschaft; annual reports 2007–2024 published. Source: https://www.abgeordnetenwatch.de/ueber-uns/mehr/finanzierung. The FAQ says about 10,919 people support it regularly (older figure) and that it runs on "einmalige und regelmäßige Spenden". Source: https://www.abgeordnetenwatch.de/ueber-uns/faq. No rule refusing party or corporate money is stated on those pages (unverified either way).

What it shows. OBSERVED: public questions to MPs and candidates; voting records ("Abstimmverhalten" with "Dafür gestimmt"/"Dagegen gestimmt"); side jobs and income table ("Nebentätigkeiten" with organisation, date registered, interval and income); research on party donations and lobbying; a "Transparenz-Versprechen" pledge, shown as "hat noch kein Transparenz-Versprechen abgegeben" when not given. Source: profile of Friedrich Merz, https://www.abgeordnetenwatch.de/profile/friedrich-merz, and https://www.abgeordnetenwatch.de/recherchen/parteispenden.

How the Q&A works and how non-answers are shown. OBSERVED: questions are moderated against a "Moderations-Codex" before publication ("die Frage wird erst mit zeitlicher Verzögerung auf der Seite veröffentlicht"); questioners must give a full name, but "seit 2021 anonymisieren aber den Nachnamen der Fragestellenden" on the public site while politicians see the full name; unanswered questions stay visible on the politician's profile; "Politiker:innen werden regelmäßig durch E-Mails vom abgeordnetenwatch.de-Team daran erinnert, dass sie unbeantworteten Fragen noch offen haben"; over 250,000 questions in 17 years, "ca. 80 Prozent beantwortet". Source: FAQ, https://www.abgeordnetenwatch.de/ueber-uns/faq. The profile header shows a blunt counter, e.g. "0 % 0 / 438 Fragen beantwortet" for the Chancellor. Source: https://www.abgeordnetenwatch.de/profile/friedrich-merz.

Response rates. OBSERVED: 2026 ranking: 14,122 questions to Bundestag MPs since 25 March 2025, 10,576 answers, 75 per cent (up from 70 per cent the year before); of 630 MPs, 381 rated "hervorragend", 28 "vorbildlich", 41 "engagiert", 178 (28.2 per cent) no rating; Thorsten Frei (CDU) answered all 861 questions; Chancellor Merz (376 questions) and Vice-Chancellor Klingbeil (257) answered "keine einzige Anfrage". Source: https://www.abgeordnetenwatch.de/bundestag/antwortranking-2026-wie-antworten-deine-abgeordneten.

Candidate engagement before elections (Kandidierendencheck). OBSERVED: for the February 2025 Bundestag election, all direct candidates got a profile ("more than 2,500 candidates across 299 electoral districts") with biography, aims, campaign images and links; a separate matching quiz (kandidierendencheck.de) of 18 theses chosen by the editorial team; "more than 64 percent of over 2,500 direct candidates" had answered by 3 February 2025 and over 69 per cent by 5 February, with answers accepted until polling day (23 February). Sources: press release, https://www.abgeordnetenwatch.de/presse/pressemitteilungen/abgeordnetenwatchde-startet-kandidierendencheck-neue-entscheidungshilfe-fuer-die-erststimme-zur-bundestagswahl; blog, https://www.abgeordnetenwatch.de/blog/wahlen/das-wahlportal-und-der-kandidierendencheck-zur-bundestagswahl-sind-gestartet. How non-answering candidates are displayed in the check is not stated on those pages (unverified).

Complaints and court cases. Not found on the pages read; unverified.

Worth borrowing (URL: https://www.abgeordnetenwatch.de/ueber-uns/faq). JUDGE: the visible "X of Y questions answered" counter, kept per mandate; unanswered questions left on the page rather than hidden; automatic email reminders; the annual public ranking that turns non-answering into a news story. All of this is non-ranking of candidates by policy; it is accountability for responsiveness.

What went wrong. JUDGE: the site's own data show the most powerful people answer least. A responsiveness metric works on backbenchers and candidates, not on leaders who can ignore it.

### 1.3 WahlSwiper / VoteSwiper

OBSERVED: started in 2017 by the studio MOVACT (Max Mitschke, Steven Siebert, Matthias Bannert); since 2021 a non-profit association "VoteSwiper", with scientific supervision by Prof. Uwe Wagschal (University of Freiburg); "wholly non-commercial", funded by donations and supporting memberships; 38 questions in 2025, swipe right/left, with short explanatory texts and videos; "27 of 29" parties answered; available in English, Turkish, Russian, Arabic, Persian since 2021. Sources: https://www.voteswiper.org/de/page/verein; Uni Freiburg, https://uni-freiburg.de/wahlswiper-jetzt-fuer-die-bundestagswahl-2025-verfuegbar/. Usage figures: not on those pages (unverified).

Worth borrowing (URL: https://uni-freiburg.de/wahlswiper-jetzt-fuer-die-bundestagswahl-2025-verfuegbar/). JUDGE: a short neutral explainer video or paragraph before each topic, and multiple languages.

### 1.4 DeinWal.de

OBSERVED: two volunteers, Tom Theile and Martin Scharm from Rostock, no funding, no party ties; compares user answers to "actual voting behavior from Bundestag roll calls" (over 100 real votes from the last term) rather than promises; questions are built from the original bill text and MPs' speeches on bundestag.de; only parties with a Bundestag record can appear; coalition parties look alike because they vote together; "Your answers stay secret and aren't sent to anyone". Source: https://btw25.deinwal.de/faq.

Worth borrowing (URL: https://btw25.deinwal.de/faq). JUDGE: the principle "what they did, not what they say" is the strongest available evidence for incumbents, and the privacy model (all computation in the browser, nothing sent) is exactly WITM's household-facts approach. Limitation to note: it cannot say anything about new candidates or parties, so it should sit beside, not instead of, quoted positions.

### 1.5 Sozial-O-Mat (Diakonie)

OBSERVED: launched by Diakonie Deutschland for the 2017 Bundestag election: 12 theses in four areas ("Familie, Flucht, Pflege und Armut"), answered by six parties (CDU/CSU, SPD, Greens, Linke, FDP, AfD), same four answer options as the Wahl-O-Mat, plus "Beispielhafte Geschichten" that "zeigen, welche Auswirkungen die verschiedenen politischen Ansätze zu den sozialen Themen für die betroffenen Menschen haben". Source: EKD, 30 Aug 2017, https://www.ekd.de/Sozial-o-Mat-Diakonie-Bundestagswahl-27961.htm. No 2025 edition: "Aufgrund des vorgezogenen Wahltermins stellt die Diakonie Deutschland keinen Sozial-O-Mat zur Verfügung", the tool could not be built in time. Source: evangelisch.de, 13 Dec 2024, https://www.evangelisch.de/inhalte/237243/13-12-2024/diakonie-kein-sozial-o-mat-fuer-bundestagswahl-2025. Usage figures: unverified.

Worth borrowing (URL: the EKD page above). JUDGE: worked examples of what each policy means for a named kind of household ("Geschichten") are the closest German cousin of WITM's household grid. The lesson from 2024/25 is that a tool that needs months of hand-made content is killed by a snap election; WITM's automated refresh jobs are the right defence.

### 1.6 Wahl-Kompass (University of Münster / Kieskompas)

OBSERVED: run by Prof. Norbert Kersting's team at the Institute for Political Science, Münster, on the Dutch Kieskompas method; launched 23 January 2025; 31 theses on a five-point scale; party self-placement is checked by political scientists who "cite supporting quotes from election manifestos with direct links"; results as a two-dimensional "Positionierungskreis" plus percentage list; used in 2019 and 2021 too. Source: Uni Münster news, https://www.uni-muenster.de/news/view.php?cmdid=14502. Usage and funding: not stated (unverified).

Worth borrowing (URL above). JUDGE: every party position carries a manifesto quotation with a link. That is the sourcing standard WITM already uses; it is reassuring that a peer-reviewed academic VAA landed on the same rule.

## 2. Netherlands

### 2.1 StemWijzer (ProDemos)

OBSERVED: parties self-place ("Partijen geven zelf aan of ze het eens of oneens zijn met de stelling, of dat ze een neutrale positie innemen") and may add a justification per statement; all parties standing in the relevant election are included unless "een partij hier zelf voor kiest" not to be; one point per matching statement, double for statements the user marks as extra important; results are not stored ("we bewaren de uitslag niet"); users may donate their answers anonymously for research, not compulsory; "StemWijzer wordt gefinancierd door ProDemos. Hier wordt geen directe subsidie voor verleend." Source: FAQ, https://source.stemwijzer.nl/faq/. Usage: "ruim 8 miljoen keer volledig ingevuld" before the 2025 Tweede Kamer election, after a record 9.1 million in 2023; 30 statements. Source: ProDemos, 29 Oct 2025, https://prodemos.nl/nieuws/stemwijzer-ruim-8-miljoen-keer-ingevuld-voor-tweede-kamerverkiezing-2025/.

Criticism. OBSERVED: in May 2010 Kieskompas founder André Krouwel accused StemWijzer of letting parties self-report without checking against programmes, "Politieke partijen zijn er op uit om onze stem te winnen", citing SP on mortgage relief, CDA on dismissal law and PvdA on student loans; StemWijzer replied that Kieskompas shows "wat André Krouwel denkt dat de partijstandpunten zouden moeten zijn". Source: NOS, 8 May 2010, https://nos.nl/l/155913.

JUDGE: this 2010 spat is the whole self-placement versus expert-coding argument in one paragraph. WITM's answer, quoting the party's or candidate's published words with date and link, avoids both failure modes: it neither lets a party fill in a form strategically nor asks a professor to guess.

### 2.2 Kieskompas

OBSERVED: established at the VU Amsterdam on 23 October 2007; 30 propositions; result shown as a point on a two-dimensional map among the parties. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kieskompas (thin article). Its German licensee describes the method as party self-placement validated by academics with manifesto quotes and links (Uni Münster page above). The site itself is JavaScript-only and could not be read here.

Criticism. OBSERVED: Simon Otjes (Stuk Rood Vlees, 1 Oct 2025) shows that 28 of the 30 items load on one left–right dimension (H = 0.81) and only two NATO items form a second one (H = 0.45), so parties line up on a diagonal and the second axis is close to redundant; changing the questions every election ("If you want to measure change, don't change what you measure") makes claimed shifts unreliable; "take the advice of Kieskompas with a grain of salt". Source: https://stukroodvlees.nl/kieskompas-nou-doet-u-het-weer/.

JUDGE: a pretty map is not evidence. A two-axis picture implies a model; if the model does not hold, the picture misleads. WITM should avoid any spatial summary of candidates.

### 2.3 CPB "Keuzes in Kaart" (official manifesto costing) and koopkrachtplaatjes

What it is. OBSERVED: the Centraal Planbureau costs the manifestos of parties that choose to take part. 2025 edition ("Keuzes in Kaart 2027–2030", published 10 October 2025): ten parties (GroenLinks-PvdA, VVD, NSC, D66, BBB, CDA, SGP, CU, Volt, JA21); it adds climate and nitrogen (with PBL) and, for the first time, "human capital", investment climate and housing effects; "Charted Choices is not an online voting advice tool" and "General elections are, of course, about much more than just economic effects". Source: CPB press release, https://www.cpb.nl/en/pressrelease/cpb-presents-its-calculations-economic-consequences-election-manifestos. The report itself says "Keuzes in Kaart is geen kieswijzer, maar maakt een onderlinge vergelijking tussen" parties possible. Source: https://www.cpb.nl/system/files/cpbmedia/CPB_Keuzes-in-Kaart-2027-2030.pdf.

How purchasing power is presented. OBSERVED: median purchasing-power change per year over 2027–2030 for all households and for five income quintiles defined against gross minimum wage (1–20 per cent: under 116 per cent of the minimum wage; 21–40 per cent: 116–184; 41–60: 184–275; 61–80: 275–405; 81–100: over 405); Table 1.4.3 medians for all households: GroenLinks-PvdA +2.3 per cent a year, VVD +0.8, NSC +1.2, D66 +1.1, BBB +0.6, CDA +0.6, SGP +0.6, ChristenUnie +1.6, Volt +1.6, JA21 +1.1; the baseline is the "basispad" (unchanged policy). The report explains that a median is "de middelste waarde van een verdeling van cijfers" and that although the economy is uncertain, "de inschatting van de richting en relatieve omvang van beleidseffecten is daar echter maar beperkt van afhankelijk". Source: the PDF above.

How assumptions are stated. OBSERVED: CPB's explainer says it uses the "statische koopkrachtdefinitie", which shows "het effect van overheidsbeleid ... op het inkomen van huishoudens, gegeven de ontwikkeling van lonen, prijzen en zorgpremies"; it excludes life events (job change, marriage, ageing) and states that "dit soort veranderingen in persoonlijke omstandigheden een groter effect op de koopkracht hebben dan beleid"; it uses medians to avoid outliers, shows spread with boxplots and scatter plots, warns that a positive median does not mean every household gains, and says the figures cannot tell you whether inequality will rise. Source: CPB, "Koopkracht", https://www.cpb.nl/koopkracht.

How parties and media criticise it. OBSERVED:
- Participation is falling: eight parties in 2023 ("steeds minder partijen laten hun plannen doorrekenen"), which CPB director Hasekamp attributed partly to "kritiek op de economische modellen"; NOS repeated the CPB line "de doorrekening is dan ook geen stemwijzer". Source: NOS, 8 Nov 2023, https://nos.nl/collectie/13960/artikel/2497079-centraal-planbureau-in-doorrekening-duidelijke-verschillen-tussen-partijen.
- 2025: the PVV (largest party) refused, saying the CPB "ignores substantial savings from asylum restrictions and civil service cuts"; the SP refused, saying its health and education plans do not fit CPB models and commissioned its own costing by independent experts. Source: NOS, https://nos.nl/artikel/2585818-verkiezingsprogramma-s-doorgelicht-wat-zijn-de-gevolgen-van-partijplannen.
- Discrepancies emerged between the programmes parties handed to the CPB and their published manifestos, "sometimes affecting billions", and viewers' main complaint was that savings fell on health care. Source: MAX Meldpunt, 20 Oct 2025, https://www.maxmeldpunt.nl/politiek/de-doorgerekende-programmas-bij-de-verkiezingen-en-bij-wie-halen-ze-het-weg-bij-de-zorg-waarom-niet-bij-de-rijken/.
- Economists argue the standard household types hide renters versus owners: housing-cost inflation differs by over five percentage points over time and "vooral lagere (midden-)inkomens kunnen hiervan de dupe zijn: zij huren vaak". Source: Cornelissen and Telling, ESB, 25 Nov 2024, https://esb.nu/koopkrachtplaatjes-onderbelichten-verschil-huurders-en-kopers/.
- Nibud (the household budget institute) computes changes for "117 Nibud-voorbeeldhuishoudens" and puts them in an online "Koopkrachtberekenaar" so that "huishoudens een beeld krijgen van de koopkrachtveranderingen die voor hen gelden"; it recalculated its September estimate (+1.3 per cent) down to +0.9 per cent in January 2026. Source: Vandaagenmorgen.nl, 1 Feb 2026, https://www.vandaagenmorgen.nl/nieuws/12795/nibud-magere-stijging-koopkracht-in-2026.
- Broader press criticism of koopkrachtplaatjes exists (Trouw opinion 18 Sep 2026 "Koopkracht zegt niets over echte welvaart"; Telegraaf 16 Sep 2026 "Koopkrachtplaatjes alweer achterhaald") but those pages were blocked or not read; content unverified.

JUDGE, what transfers to WITM. The CPB is the closest thing in Europe to WITM's "what would a win change for a household like yours", and its practice suggests five rules. (1) Say what the number is: a median over a period, under a static definition, against a stated baseline. (2) Say what it leaves out, in one sentence ("changes in your own life usually matter more than policy"). (3) Show spread, not just the middle. (4) Never let a party's submission differ from its published manifesto without saying so; WITM quotes the published document, which is the right side of that problem. (5) Expect refusals and attacks on the model from parties that come out badly; the defence is a public method and a public ledger, which WITM already has.

## 3. Switzerland

### 3.1 smartvote (Politools)

Who and funding. OBSERVED: a project of the "politically neutral, non-profit association" Politools, running since 2003, "over 350 elections"; team led by Daniel Schwarz and Jan Fivaz; "mainly financed by contributions from political parties and candidates" plus paid data services to media partners, foundation grants, user donations and unpaid volunteer work; a federal-election year is a "fat" year whose surplus funds the three "lean" years. Sources: https://www.smartvote.ch/en/project-and-team; https://www.smartvote.ch/en/funding; https://www.smartvote.ch/en/faq. Candidates accept a "Preismodell" and a "Teilnahmegebühr" (amount not stated; can be waived for some elections); parties or list managers can register candidates in bulk. Source: candidate terms, https://blog.smartvote.ch/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/sv25_agb_de.pdf.

What it shows and how. OBSERVED: candidates, not parties, answer the questionnaire ("between 45 and 75 questions, depending on the selection"); the smartvote team alone writes the questions, drawing on government sites, party platforms, interest groups, media and public submissions (over 1,500 proposals before 2019); an adaptive questionnaire picks the next question from earlier answers; results rank candidates by "city block" distance (Euclidean before 2025) and the FAQ warns that a 70 per cent match is "a geometric measure of agreement", not 70 per cent identical answers; the "smartspider" plots eight thematic axes (0–100) and the "smartmap" a two-dimensional position; weighting affects the ranking but not the graphics. Sources: https://www.smartvote.ch/en/questionnaire; https://www.smartvote.ch/en/faq; https://www.smartvote.ch/en/voting-advice.

Candidate participation. OBSERVED: federal elections 2023: National Council "83% von 5925 Kandidierenden" had a profile; Council of States "85% von 184"; second round "79% von 33"; "Wahlempfehlungen erstellt 2,303,169". Source: https://www.smartvote.ch/de/election_groups/527. The home page claims "85% der Kandidierenden besitzen ein smartvote-Profil" (2023, national and cantonal) and "20% der Wähler/-innen benutzen smartvote". Source: https://www.smartvote.ch/de/home.

Non-answering candidates. OBSERVED: candidates who have not "completed and confirmed the questionnaire in full will therefore not be included" (FAQ). ## 5. Belgium: De Stemtest (VRT / De Standaard) and RTBF Test électoral

OBSERVED (Flemish version): made by VRT NWS and De Standaard, developed by the University of Antwerp (Prof. Stefaan Walgrave) and UCLouvain (Prof. Benoît Rihoux); 35 statements across three levels of government, distilled from "250 stellingen" checked against party programmes; criteria: forward-looking, clearly worded, tells parties apart, likely to matter in the campaign; statements where all parties agree are dropped; parties answered the 35 statements themselves, and the researchers weighted statements by "hoeveel woorden en zinnen de partijen aan elk thema besteden"; seven parties (N-VA, Vlaams Belang, CD&V, Open VLD, Vooruit, Groen, PVDA) qualified under the rules "verkozenen in 1 van de parlementen", "overal lijsten indienen" and a "omstandig partijprogramma" that is "te checken"; the party Voor U was excluded because only "10 procent van de stellingen" could be answered from its programme; "De Stemtest is al meer dan 6 miljoen keer uitgevoerd" (both languages; 2019: 4.4 million, only 15 per cent abandoned); result is a ranked list, with "5 extra vragen" offered when parties are close; "Het resultaat van De Stemtest is in elk geval geen stemadvies". ## 6. France

### 6.1 Elyze (2022)

OBSERVED: a Tinder-style app launched January 2022 by students Grégoire Cazcarra and François Mari with Gaspard Guermonprez and others from the civic movement Les Engagés; users swiped on anonymised proposals from presidential candidates and got a top-three ranking; "over 1 million users in less than 2 weeks" and over 2 million downloads by mid-April 2022; early versions collected date of birth, postcode, gender and voting intention; when a user accepted every proposal the tie-break showed Macron first (a default-ordering bug, later alphabetical, then explicit tie handling); on 26 January security researcher Mathis Hammel showed candidate data were "readable and writable" and added fake proposals; the developers deleted personal data, stopped collecting and open-sourced the code. Source: fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elyze as returned by the fetch tool on 25 Sep 2026 (note: the live article now appears to have been deleted and the summary came from a cached copy; treat the details as secondary). Corroboration: Usine Digitale, 19–20 Jan 2022, "Face aux accusations, l'application Elyze supprime les données personnelles de ses utilisateurs", https://www.usine-digitale.fr/article/face-aux-accusations-l-application-elyze-supprime-les-donnees-personnelles-de-ses-utilisateurs.N1774537 — which records the data collected (birth date, gender, postcode, answers, voting intentions), a privacy clause allowing transfer to "tiers", hosting on AWS (Cloud Act concern), closed source, the tie-break favouring Macron, a CNIL examination with possible fines up to "20 millions d'euros ou 4% du chiffre d'affaires annuel mondial", "un million de téléchargements", and the decision to delete data and publish the code. JUDGE: Elyze is the clearest cautionary tale in this review. Collecting birth date, postcode and voting intention from a million people in a week, with a vague third-party clause, from an app whose ranking had an undisclosed tie-break, was three separate mistakes; the fix (delete, stop collecting, open the code) is the right one and is WITM's starting position.

## 14. Quality standards and data protection

### 14.1 The Lausanne Declaration and academic guidance

OBSERVED: research on VAAs is coordinated by the ECPR Research Network on Voting Advice Applications, convened by Stefan Marschall and Diego Garzia since 2015 (HHU Düsseldorf, http://vaa-research.net/ now redirects to the university page; ECPR group page, https://ecpr.eu/group/voting-advice-applications). German Wikipedia records that within this network "entstand 2014 auch die sogenannte 'Lausanne Declaration', welche Standards und Mindestanforderungen an diese Anwendungen formuliert". Source: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wahlentscheidungshilfe. The English Wikipedia VAA article adds the University of Antwerp recommendation of "careful selection of VAA statements and a proper process of benchmarking based on survey data", and effect studies (Finland 2003: 3 per cent changed vote; Netherlands 2003: 10 per cent; Belgium: 1 per cent actual change). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voting_advice_application.

The text of the Declaration itself could not be retrieved today (publisher pages returned 403; unverified). From memory it asks VAA makers to publish their method, funding and party-placement procedure, to invite every contender on the ballot, to protect user data, and to make data available for research; treat that list as unverified until the ECPR Press appendix (Garzia and Marschall, eds, "Matching Voters with Parties and Candidates", 2014) is checked. I found no Council of Europe instrument specific to VAAs; unverified.

### 14.2 GDPR handling of answers, as practised

OBSERVED, from the pages above:
- Nothing stored server-side: SVT ("SVT sparar ingen persondata kopplad till hur enskilda besökare svarat", local storage only); DeinWal ("aren't sent to anyone"); Latarnik ("nie gromadzi, ani nie zapisuje"); Decidir23J ("no personal information").
- Stored briefly for debugging: DR (anonymised logs "slettes automatisk efter 3 dage").
- Stored but anonymised, with opt-in research donation: StemWijzer (keeps only the eens/oneens distribution, research sharing "niet verplicht"); Yle (answers cannot be linked to IP or login; 2019 candidate answers opened as CC data with names removed).
- Stored in cookies with an identifier, research in aggregate: Volební kalkulačka (90-day cookie).
- Stored with analytics and cross-visit linking, sensitive-data acknowledgement, GDPR and Swiss law cited: smartvote (logs deleted after 14 days; "besonders schützenswerte Daten").
- Went wrong: Elyze (birth date, postcode, voting intention, third-party clause, CNIL involvement, then deletion).

JUDGE: political opinion is special-category data under GDPR Article 9, and none of the well-regarded tools tries to hold it in identifiable form. WITM's browser-only household facts and no-account design are already at the strict end of this spectrum; write that down on /about/data-use in the same plain terms SVT and DR use.

## 16. The 15 most useful lessons for What's It To Me? (which does not match or rank)

1. Publish the inclusion rule before launch and apply it to everyone on the ballot. Wahl-O-Mat lost in court over an eight-party cap and settled on "all parties, one click". https://www.bpb.de/die-bpb/presse/pressemitteilungen/291808/wahl-o-mat-zur-europawahl-wieder-online/
2. If anyone on the ballot is missing from a page, say so on that page. The Brussels court made VRT state that Voor U was not included, on pain of a daily fine. https://www.bruzz.be/actua/politiek/els-ampe-wint-rechtzaak-tegen-vrt-over-niet-opname-de-stemtest-2024-05-28
3. Never launch a candidate list that looks complete but is not. Yle was reprimanded because users were not told the tool was incomplete before official nominations. Date-stamp "provisional" and log when the list became official. https://yle.fi/a/74-20210094
4. Show the source next to every position, as Wahl-Kompass and EU&I do with manifesto quotes and links. This is what lets an expert-coded tool defend itself. https://www.uni-muenster.de/news/view.php?cmdid=14502 and https://euandi.eu/en/faq.html
5. When a candidate has not confirmed something, label it. Latarnik marks unconfirmed positions "nieoficjalne"; NRK says in writing when it overruled a party. https://latarnikwyborczy.pl/ and https://www.nrk.no/norge/slik-har-vi-laga-valomatane-og-partiguiden-2025-1.17416882
6. Show non-response as a fact, not a blank. Abgeordnetenwatch's "0 / 438 Fragen beantwortet" and SVT's start-page list of parties that declined make silence visible without scoring policy. https://www.abgeordnetenwatch.de/profile/friedrich-merz and https://www.svt.se/nyheter/inrikes/fragor-och-svar-om-svts-valkompasser
7. Freeze confirmed answers and publish the correction route. smartvote: "Nach erfolgter Bestätigung sind die Antworten nicht mehr veränderbar", corrections by email if "nachvollziehbar und ausreichend begründet". WITM's ledger is the natural place to record every change. https://blog.smartvote.ch/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/sv25_agb_de.pdf
8. For the household impact, copy the CPB's honesty about the number: static definition, stated baseline, median with spread, and the sentence that personal changes "een groter effect op de koopkracht hebben dan beleid". https://www.cpb.nl/koopkracht
9. Show many household types, not one average. Nibud runs 117 example households and a calculator where people find "de koopkrachtveranderingen die voor hen gelden". Renters and owners differ (ESB). https://www.vandaagenmorgen.nl/nieuws/12795/nibud-magere-stijging-koopkracht-in-2026 and https://esb.nu/koopkrachtplaatjes-onderbelichten-verschil-huurders-en-kopers/
10. Pair "what they say" with "what they did". DeinWal and KohoVolit use real roll-call votes; WITM already pulls Parliament APIs, so incumbents' votes on the topics quoted belong on the same page, clearly labelled as record not promise. https://btw25.deinwal.de/faq and https://www.volebnikalkulacka.cz/o-projektu
11. Build the encyclopaedia page, not the quiz. NRK's Partiguiden is "eit slags oppslagsverk": one issue, one question, two to four answer options, parties sorted across them, sources at the foot, parties read it before publication. That is a non-ranking comparison design proven at national scale. https://www.nrk.no/norge/slik-har-vi-laga-valomatane-og-partiguiden-2025-1.17416882
12. Tell worked stories. Diakonie's Sozial-O-Mat added "Beispielhafte Geschichten" of how each approach lands on real people; WITM's household grid can be narrated the same way. https://www.ekd.de/Sozial-o-Mat-Diakonie-Bundestagswahl-27961.htm
13. Local beats national. SVT's growth in 2026 came from 311 local compasses (+80 per cent) built by twice as many journalists; Yle writes local questions for every municipality. WITM's council pages and May 2027 plan are pointed the right way. https://omoss.svt.se/arkiv/nyhetsarkiv/2026-09-14-fler-an-37-miljoner-genomforda-valkompasser-hos-svt.html and https://yle.fi/a/74-20147441
14. Write the privacy promise in one line and mean it. SVT: nothing linked to individual answers, local storage only; DR: debug logs deleted after three days; Yle: nothing linkable to IP or login. Put an equivalent sentence on /about/data-use. https://www.dr.dk/nyheder/politik/folketingsvalg/hvordan-har-i-lavet-den-syv-spoergsmaal-og-svar-om-kandidattesten
15. Be named, and open the data. Anonymous tools (MonVote2027, Partiguiden.com) get attacked on identity; Yle released candidate answers as CC data and Volební kalkulačka publishes its analytics. WITM's /who-we-are, /data and /ledger are the right instincts; add a licence and an archive plan so the site does not end like VoteWatch. https://www.faktisk.no/artikkel/partikandidat-star-bak-anonym-valgomat/126080 and https://yle.fi/a/3-10725384

## 17. Mistakes to avoid

- Collecting anything identifying alongside political answers. Elyze gathered birth date, postcode and voting intention from a million people and had to delete it under CNIL scrutiny. https://www.usine-digitale.fr/article/face-aux-accusations-l-application-elyze-supprime-les-donnees-personnelles-de-ses-utilisateurs.N1774537
- A writable or unreviewed data store. Elyze's candidate data were "readable and writable" by outsiders. WITM's append-only ledger and moderated /candidates/submit are the antidote; keep write paths few and logged.
- Hidden tie-breaks and default orders. Elyze's default ordering put one candidate first when scores tied. WITM's ballot-paper order is defensible precisely because it is the official order; never let any sort be silent.
- Capping who is shown for tidiness (Wahl-O-Mat 2019).
- Excluding a contender without a written, checkable rule (VRT 2024).
- Launching on a provisional candidate list without saying so (Yle 2025–26).
- Letting a party's submission differ from its published programme without flagging it (CPB 2025 discrepancies, "sometimes affecting billions"). https://www.maxmeldpunt.nl/politiek/de-doorgerekende-programmas-bij-de-verkiezingen-en-bij-wie-halen-ze-het-weg-bij-de-zorg-waarom-niet-bij-de-rijken/
- Presenting a model output as a fact about one household. CPB's own explainer says personal circumstances usually outweigh policy; renters versus owners are hidden in standard household types.
- Spatial pictures with an unproven model (Kieskompas per Otjes 2025). https://stukroodvlees.nl/kieskompas-nou-doet-u-het-weer/
- Equal-weight "conflict filters" that hide consensus (Euronews on Wahl-O-Mat). A non-ranking site can show where candidates agree as well as where they differ.
- Hand-built content that cannot survive a snap election (Sozial-O-Mat 2025). https://www.evangelisch.de/inhalte/237243/13-12-2024/diakonie-kein-sozial-o-mat-fuer-bundestagswahl-2025
- Depending on party or candidate fees for survival (smartvote's "fat year / lean years" model). https://www.smartvote.ch/en/funding
- Anonymity of the makers (MonVote2027; Partiguiden.com built by a candidate).
- Leaving no exit plan for the archive (VoteWatch Europe). https://www.votewatch.eu/

## 18. How the high-participation tools get candidates to take part, and what transfers to a UK site with no media partner

What they do (OBSERVED, sources above):

smartvote (83 per cent of 5,925 National Council candidates, 2023). Candidates and parties pay to take part, so parties have a financial and reputational stake; list managers register whole slates in bulk; the questionnaire is the only route to a profile, so absence is conspicuous; profiles stay up at least a month after the election and are sold back to media as data; answers are frozen on confirmation, which makes the exercise feel official. Twenty-two years of continuity mean candidates expect it.

Yle vaalikone (about 90 per cent, 2019; 70 per cent within a day, 2023; 14,000+ in 2025). The public broadcaster's reach makes the vaalikone the default place voters look, especially under-35s; local questions written by regional newsrooms make it relevant to every municipality; the tool opens early and keeps accepting answers ("uusia ehdokkaita tulee vielä mukaan"); answers are released as open data; and Finland's open-list system means every candidate competes personally for preference votes, so a free profile is worth having.

SVT Valkompass (2,792 Riksdag candidates wrote their own answers; 100 per cent of regional parties; 97 per cent of 2,167 local parties, 2026). Every candidate on every list is invited with verified contact details and a secure login; 7,000 emails and 10,000 answer versions were handled and moderated by a dedicated desk; a full year of preparation; twice the journalists on local compasses.

Abgeordnetenwatch (64–69 per cent of 2,500+ direct candidates within days, 2025; 75 per cent of 14,122 questions to MPs). Every direct candidate gets a profile whether or not they respond, so the only choice is between a full profile and a visibly empty one; the profile persists into the parliamentary term and becomes the MP's public record; automatic email reminders; an annual public ranking of responsiveness that newspapers report; 12,500 donors give it independence and staying power.

DR Kandidattest, Latarnik, NRK: candidates must answer everything to appear (DR); positions are filled from documents and marked "unofficial" if the party stays silent (Latarnik); the broadcaster proposes a placement and the party is invited to correct it (NRK). In each case the tool proceeds without the party, and says so.

What transfers to WITM (JUDGE). Six mechanisms do not need a broadcaster or money:

1. Profile-by-default. Create every candidate's page from Democracy Club, ElectionLeaflets and public records before asking for anything. The candidate's choice is then between a page built from their published words and the same page with their own confirmation on it. This is Abgeordnetenwatch's and NRK's lever and WITM already has the data.
2. The "not yet confirmed" flag. A visible, neutral label on each quotation ("not yet confirmed by the candidate") plus a one-click confirm-or-correct link in the invitation email. Latarnik's "nieoficjalne" and NRK's disclosed overrides show that filling from documents is acceptable if labelled.
3. Bulk routes through agents and local parties. smartvote registers via list managers; SVT reached 97 per cent of local parties by email. In the UK the election agent named on the Statement of Persons Nominated is the equivalent contact; local party officers are the bulk route for May 2027.
4. Reminders on a schedule, and a public responsiveness count. Abgeordnetenwatch's "X of Y answered" is compatible with never ranking on policy. WITM could show "confirmed 12 of 14 published positions" without any score.
5. Open early, keep accepting, timestamp everything. Yle and DR say the tool improves until polling day; the JSN case shows the cost of not saying so. WITM's ledger already timestamps; make the page-level "last confirmed" date visible.
6. Local relevance and small-party equality. NRK: "Eit lite parti har like god sjanse til å vinne som eit stort"; SVT's growth came from local compasses. In a 15-candidate by-election with many small parties and independents, the promise of an identical page in ballot order is itself the recruiting pitch: for most of them it is the only place they will appear beside the big parties on equal terms.

What does not transfer: paid participation (smartvote), broadcast promotion, and open-list preference voting. UK candidates in first-past-the-post seats have weaker personal incentives than Finnish or Swiss ones, so the profile-by-default and public confirmation count matter more, not less.
