# What sits between your council and Westminster?

**Date:** 24 September 2026
**Question:** For any UK postcode, what is the "regional" level between the council and Westminster, what does it decide, and is it elected?

**Summary**
1. There is no single "regional" level. England has mayors and combined authorities in some places only. London has its own Mayor and Assembly. Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have their own parliaments instead.
2. Some things in between are elected: mayors, police and crime commissioners, the three devolved parliaments and the London Assembly. Many are not: health boards, fire authorities in most places, national park authorities, Welsh joint committees, Scottish police and fire boards.
3. postcodes.io gives most of the geography the site needs, but not combined authorities, Northern Ireland council areas or Scottish community councils. Democracy Club only lists an election once it has been called. So the site will need its own calendar of when each level is next due.

---

## How to read this paper

- "Elected?" means **directly elected by voters**. A body run by councillors that other councillors chose is marked "No (appointed)".
- Every date is correct as of the source date shown. Devolution in England is changing month by month. Where we could not check something, we say **unverified**.
- We looked up six real postcodes on postcodes.io and on the Democracy Club elections API on 24 September 2026. Four of them (M60 2LA, G2 1DU, BT1 5GS and WC1H 9JE) belong to big public buildings (town or city halls), not homes. They still show the right areas.

## What this means for the site

These are options, not recommendations about any candidate or party.

1. **"Regional" means a different thing in each nation.** One neutral way to show it: a row called "Between your council and Westminster". It would show whatever applies to that postcode: a combined-authority mayor, the GLA, the Scottish Parliament, the Senedd or the NI Assembly. Some English postcodes have **no mayor at all**: for example Kent or Surrey (not in the ONS list or the 2026 orders), and Devon or Lancashire (in a combined county authority that has no mayor). One option is to say so plainly rather than leave a gap (a choice for Romily and Barny). Romily answered "I don't know" on this. The choice between (a) showing only bodies you can vote for, or (b) also listing unelected ones like ICBs and fire authorities, is a product decision for Romily and Barny.
2. **The site needs its own small table** for things neither API gives: council → combined authority (start from the ONS Dec 2025 lookup and add Cumbria, Cheshire and Warrington, Sussex and Brighton, Hampshire and the Solent); who runs police and fire in each area; and the **next scheduled election date** for each level (from the law, as in this paper).
3. **Use two postcodes.io lookups for Scotland.** The main lookup returns nothing for the Scottish Parliament.
4. **Northern Ireland needs Democracy Club for council areas.** postcodes.io gives the ward, but people vote by district electoral area.
5. **Things to re-check before May 2027:**
   - The PCC abolition bill (not yet published on 18 Aug 2026).
   - The council-reorganisation review from 7 Sep 2026. It decides whether Lincolnshire and others elect old or new councils in May 2027.
   - Whether Greater Essex and Norfolk and Suffolk authorities are set up.
   - Any new PCC transfers to mayors under the 2026 Act.
   - ICB mergers in April 2027.
6. **For the Holborn and St Pancras test on 8 Oct 2026**, only the Westminster ballot is live. The page could still show the other levels (Camden, the Mayor of London and the London Assembly) as "not voting this time; next due May 2030 / May 2028".
