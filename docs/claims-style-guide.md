# Claims style guide

Every claim is a quotation from a named, dated, linked source, with a short summary as a reading aid. The quotation is the record.

## Threshold-precision rule
A summary may not use "more", "fewer", "strengthen", "tackle", "improve", "cut", "boost", "protect", "invest" or similar without the number, date or concrete object taken from the source ("recruit 6,500 more teachers", not "recruit more teachers"). If the source itself is vague, the claim is filed with `precision = aspiration` and rendered with the label "Stated aim, no measurable commitment found".

## Layer chips (fixed set, grey, identical weight, no order implied)
`Manifesto` · `Enacted record` · `Candidate statement` · `Campaign leaflet` · `Third-party analysis`

Legend shown wherever chips appear: "Candidate statements and leaflets are published as written; we check they are real, not that they are true."

## Wording
- Neutral verbs: "says", "pledged", "states". Never "admits", "claims" (in the pejorative sense), "boasts", "fails to".
- The party or candidate's own name for their policy where one exists ("Cheap Power Plan"), in quotation marks on first use.
- Conditions in plain words in `applies_if`, never in the summary.
- One claim per commitment. Do not bundle.

## Sources
- Primary first: the party's or candidate's own publication, the official record, the statute. Press reports only as a flagged interim (`notes` says so) until the primary is found.
- `published_on` is the source's own date; `retrieved_at` is when we fetched it. Both are shown.

## Corrections
Never edit a row. Insert a superseding row with the reason; the change log is public.
