Trust without scoring

You said pledged-vs-delivered data could show how trustworthy a candidate or party is, as long as it is purely factual and applied to everyone equally.

Showing "pledged X in 2024; voted against X in 2025, source" for every incumbent is a fact. Turning that into "kept 7 of 10 promises" is a number, and numbers get compared, and a comparison is a ranking. Which do you want?

Who is behind this

You said the owners should be anonymous so nobody reads bias into the app.

Every credible tool in the worldwide scan names who runs it: a civic-education agency in Germany, a university in Ireland, a newsroom in New Zealand. UK law already requires some campaign material to say who is behind it, and a bill in the Lords would widen that. The first question a journalist asks about an election tool is "who made this and who pays for it", and "we won't say" is the worst possible answer. Impartiality can come from open process (published rules, open code, public change log) rather than hidden founders. Does that change your view? If you still want distance, what would you accept: a named not-for-profit with trustees, a named advisory board, founders named but not front-and-centre?
