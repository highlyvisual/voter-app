Round three.

Much has been built since round two: the site is live at voter-app-uk.netlify.app with 28 elections, 190 sourced positions, maps, photos, a parliamentary record panel and the comparison views. The name

Barny chose This Shit Matters and the domain thisshitmatters.org, and it is live. Your shortlist was WhereYouStand, WhatChanges, Impact. The strong case for his: memorable, honest about the mission. The cost: school filters, some partner meetings (Democracy Club, mySociety, the Electoral Commission), and any admissions reader. It is a one-minute change either way.

Party colours

Your round-one rule was no symbolically loaded colour anywhere. Barny reversed it: candidates now carry their party's established colour (Labour red, Tory blue, and so on) on their mark, a pill, and the comparison columns. It is identification only, at identical weight; parties without an established colour stay grey. The interface itself uses no party-adjacent colour.

Photos

Barny's own build brief set an all-or-none rule: show photos only when every candidate on a ballot has one, so nobody is visually disadvantaged. He then asked for photos wherever available, and that is what is live: 116 of 138 candidates have one on Democracy Club; the rest show initials, and each page says a missing photo means nothing about the candidate.

The map marker

After a postcode lookup, the page shows the voting-area boundary and an approximate circle at the centre of the postcode district (NW1, not NW1 2DB). Only the district travels to the page; the full postcode is discarded in the lookup and nothing is stored. It is still the first time anything about the visitor's location reaches a page.

The place panel

A fuller place panel is possible: conservation areas, Article 4 directions and (where councils publish it) planning applications near an address, from planning.data.gov.uk. It needs a coarse location, about 100 metres, to be carried from the postcode step to the ballot page. That is finer than the district circle above.

The archive and hindsight

The 2024 general election in this seat is on the site as an archive, shown as it stood at the time. Labour's enacted record since (the two-child limit, the Renters' Rights Act) exists on the by-election page as dated, sourced positions. Showing it on the archive page too would let people compare "what they said" with "what they did", which is the pledged-versus-record idea from round one, but it edges toward hindsight on a page that promises none.

Identity questions

The household form now has optional yes/no questions: disability or long-term condition, unpaid carer, visa or asylum, means-tested benefit, drives, armed-forces service. Blank answers are never assumed either way. Sexual orientation, gender identity, religion and ethnicity are deliberately not asked; equality positions are shown to everyone instead. Our recommendation is to keep it that way.

Welsh

Four of the 28 current elections are in Wales or Scotland. A Welsh version of the fixed text and the guides is planned, and the strings are listed for a translator. We should not ship a machine translation.
